# One Billion Tokens

Between personal and professional use, I consumed over a billion tokens in January of 2026, not inclusive of non-metered AI services. I moved from skepticism to rational exuberance in December 2025, and I am not unique. Opus 4.5 and Gemini 3 demonstrated a level of quality output not yet seen in the wild, leading many, myself included, to re-evaluate our assumptions.

Inspired by Gas Town, I was able to combine December's work on context engineering with a multi-agent approach to achieve a level of output quality that I consider acceptable for production.

Combining streamlined developer experience tooling powered by Nix and devenv, incremental loading for MCP servers, and a multi-agent approach to design, implementation, and QA, my agents are now able to produce high quality code with minimal oversight. This is inclusive of both backend and frontend changes, and anything that gets me away from copy-pasting build errors and console output into an agent context is welcome.

I am happy to have implemented this in a model and platform agnostic way. We see this in-product now with Claude Code agent teams, and I am excited to see how this evolves.

I am empathetic to software engineers who feel devalued by this technology, but I have not experienced the same personal crisis that some have. I never found the actual process of writing code to be particularly enjoyable. Writing code, or a DSL, or YAML, was always a means to an end. Coming from an infrastructure heavy background, I worried that my lack of software engineering experience would be the blocker to my own ability. These tools, given the right harness, have enabled me to implement a combined product, infrastructure, and software engineering vision I never thought I would achieve. I can both learn and implement frameworks and languages I have not used, or not used heavily, at a rate I never thought possible.

This brought a joy back to computing that I thought I had lost. Between work and fatherhood, I have less time for personal projects than ever, and I missed the creative and intellectual stimulation. Now, just a couple of hours in an evening can produce a functional ESP32 or SDR project, cyber-physical system around the house, or MagTag toy for the kids. Far from diminishing my understanding, I now spend more time learning and understanding the underlying systems and less time fiddling with the code itself.

I am excited to see how this area evolves and I am cautiously optimistic about the future.

I have a hard time treating democratization of creation as negative. It's time to build.

  - Be more present in 1:1s and meetings without sacrifice.

  - Share the recording and transcript with others.
  - Use the transcript to rapidly create concrete work products.
